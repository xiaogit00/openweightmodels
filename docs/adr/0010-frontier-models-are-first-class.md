# Frontier closed models are first-class in the catalogue

The product compares open-weight models against frontier closed models, so both are Model registry entries that can serve as a Base model or a Challenger. The comparison is the point: an author arrives with a chat from Claude or GPT and wants to see what the open-weight models do with the same conversation, or the reverse. Every entry records whether its weights are openly available, and the interface shows that distinction wherever a Model is chosen or named. The name OpenWeightModels stays; "open weight" describes the catalogue's founding half, not a restriction on what may be compared.

**Considered options**: keeping the catalogue open-weight only and rejecting frontier models (rejected: it removes the most interesting comparison and makes the Claude exports this feature was designed around unpublishable); allowing frontier models only as Base models (rejected: attribution becomes asymmetric and they could never be replayed, so the platform could not generate their side); a separate closed-model catalogue and picker (rejected: same Job pipeline, same Response shape, same reading experience — two catalogues would duplicate all of it for a label).

**Consequences**: the tagline, glossary and product copy say "open-weight and frontier", and the Board can eventually filter on the distinction. Closed models are reachable only through their vendor or an aggregator, so availability, versions and pricing move on someone else's schedule, and self-hosting is impossible. The registry needs a curation rule that covers both classes. Picker and card UI must make the distinction visible, because a reader should never assume a closed model's weights were available.
